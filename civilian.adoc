== deep night thought

Sometimes I think I’m just another fucking civilian, you know? Just a nobody, stuck in this messed-up world while everything falls apart. Wars all over the place, climate collapse, overpopulation—it’s like we’re all racing to burn it all down. And here I am, sitting with these stupid, utopian daydreams, thinking maybe—just maybe—there’s another way. And yeah, it sounds dumb, like a high, late-night thought, but when the world makes you this goddamn sad, it’s hard not to dream.

I keep wondering, what if we just stopped? Not the governments or the armies—us. The regular people, the civilians. What if we just walked out of all of it? Literally walked. Like, imagine this wild, global pilgrimage, no banners or slogans, just people. Starting right where we are—wherever we live—and we’d just go. We’d pass through Jerusalem, through Mecca, through the war zones, Gaza, Ukraine, Tehran. And we’d keep going. No sides, no borders, just this massive flow of people, a human tide walking for peace. Walking for something real, something that doesn’t involve killing each other.

It sounds crazy, I know. But I can’t shake it. Like Gandhi walking for salt, but this time it’s us walking for sanity, for survival. For life, man. I keep thinking of this Camino de Santiago, but reversed. We start where we are and just keep walking—through all the wreckage, through the world we’ve ruined, trying to fix it step by step. A pilgrimage not for some god or redemption, but for us. For the people. For what’s left of humanity. 

And hey, if there’s still any bars open along the way, throw open the doors. We’ll need food, we’ll need a drink, we’ll need to laugh. We’ll need to feel human. Because even while we’re marching through all this shit, we’ll find moments of joy. Of love. The real kind, not the cheesy, idealistic stuff. But the love we all want so badly and can’t admit.

Do I even have the guts for this? Probably not. It’s terrifying. But man, I’m so damn sick of it all. We’re just civilians. Just fucking civilians. But maybe, just maybe, we’re all we’ve got. And maybe that’s enough.
